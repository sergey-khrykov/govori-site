Answers at a glance

most looked-up word forward, SR → gloss
biti v. “to be” — 70 distinct users tapped it from search, nearly 30% ahead of ići (54). The entire forward top 10 is verbs.
most looked-up word reverse, gloss → SR
ići v. “to go” (22 users), then jesti, biti, naći. Top typed Russian queries: идти and привет (18 users each); top English: there, man (4 each).
search language
Russian glosses carry 87% of all terminal searches (46,263 vs 6,879 English). Both audiences split the same way: two-thirds forward, one-third reverse.
time of day CET
Evening 18–23 h holds 36% of searching, midday 12–17 h another 35%. Peak hour is 18:00; there is a genuine after-midnight tail.
parts of speech
Nouns take 41% of result taps, verbs 37% — but verbs own the head of the distribution. Most-tapped noun: posao; adjective: dobar; adverb: ipak.
most favorited
ipak, otići, čuti — 6 users each. 2,201 adds spread so thin that no word reached 7 people: favoriting is idiosyncratic, not consensual.

Usage notes — read before quoting any number

  1. July is iOS-only; Android exists in the data only from Aug 13. An analytics-export misconfiguration (since fixed) kept Android usage out of the record until then. Never read Android’s smaller totals as lower engagement, and never trend Android month-over-month.
  2. “Most looked-up” is measured by result taps, not card opens. Both apps’ onboarding plants a showcase word into recents — ljubav got 96 card opens (49 Android users in August) but only 7 users ever reached it via search. Tap counts are immune; card-open counts are contaminated for the 16 showcase words.
  3. Searches are logged per keystroke, so query counts use the terminal-query collapse (a query is dropped if a later one in the same session extends it). Many surviving queries are 1–2-letter prefixes — users tap results mid-typing — so typed-query rankings below are filtered to ≥3 characters.
  4. Counting unit is distinct users, not events, wherever a ranking is shown — one enthusiast tapping biti 46 times in July would otherwise own the chart.
  5. Window: Jul 3 – Aug 30 (July 1–2 aged out of retention; Aug 31 not yet exported). Hours are Europe/Belgrade — right for ~80% of users, see audience below.

Verbs own the head. All ten of the forward top 10 are verbs — and 40 of the top 50. People don’t open biti to learn what “to be” means — they come for sam · si · je · smo, the conjugation. The high-frequency lookup is inflection anxiety, not meaning. The reverse list is more mixed (32 verbs of 50): deeper down, concrete daily life surfaces — pas, mačka, čajnik, baterija, vruć — the words you need when the kettle breaks or the dog needs a vet, which is exactly what a reverse dictionary is for.

The Russian list reads like the first week of settling in: greeting people (привет, спасибо, до свидания), moving around (идти, ехать), the household (дом, лампа, одеяло, полотенце), the August weather (жарко, жара), orienting in time (вчера, завтра, сегодня, уже). Question 3 · time of day

When the dictionary gets opened

All 178k raw search events by hour, Europe/Belgrade time. The dictionary is an afternoon-and-evening tool: usage climbs through the morning, plateaus after lunch, and peaks at 18:00 — then holds up past midnight.

Searches by hour · CET

hover a bar for exact counts

The midnight tail is real: 5,290 searches in the 00:00 hour — twice the 01:00 volume and more than any hour before 08:00. A late-evening study habit that spills across midnight, not an artifact.

Context · who is looking

The audience behind the numbers

Active users by country · August

of 1,475 user-rows; both platforms

Serbia itself is 56% of usage, with Russia second. Put beside the 87% Russian-gloss share, the picture is coherent: the core audience is Russian speakers living in Serbia, learning the language of the place they moved to. Montenegro and Slovenia round out the region; the diaspora (US, UK, NL, DE) is a visible but thin tail — and it is where most of the English-gloss searching plausibly lives.
